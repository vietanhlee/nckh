\subsection{Learning Stability and Convergence Analysis}

To analyze the training dynamic behavior and noise resilience of the graph architectures, Figure~\ref{fig:compare_mae_curves} plots the validation MAE curves across training epochs for a representative random seed.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig/fig_compare_mae_curve_models_graph.png}
\caption{Comparison of Validation MAE convergence curves across training epochs between STGCN Baseline and TA-STGCN (Our Model) for a representative seed.}
\label{fig:compare_mae_curves}
\end{figure}

The convergence curves in Figure~\ref{fig:compare_mae_curves} demonstrate that \textbf{TA-STGCN produces a significantly smoother, less oscillatory validation MAE curve} compared to the STGCN Baseline. From a theoretical perspective, this enhanced convergence stability is driven by three underlying architectural mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global Temporal Noise Filtering via Attention:} Standard STGCN relies solely on 1D Gated Temporal Convolutions (GLU) with fixed local receptive fields ($K_t=3$, corresponding to 15 minutes). When real-world camera feeds experience non-stationary temporal noise—such as sudden traffic signal changes or brief visual camera glitches—local 1D convolutions propagate these high-frequency fluctuations directly into loss updates, causing sharp validation spikes. In contrast, TA-STGCN places Model-Level Multi-Head Temporal Self-Attention ($h=4$) post-ST-Conv blocks. Self-attention computes dynamic temporal affinity weights $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{in}} \times T_{\text{in}}}$ across the full 24-step (120-minute) historical window, acting as a \emph{dynamic low-pass filter} that automatically down-weights transient noise while preserving long-term momentum.
    \item \textbf{Gradient Stabilization via LayerNorm and Residual Connections:} Within TA-STGCN's Transformer attention block, Layer Normalization stabilizes activation distributions across mini-batches ($Z^{(1)} = \text{LayerNorm}(Z + \text{MHA}(Z))$), preventing internal covariate shift and smoothing the loss surface during Adam optimization.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Aspect Representation via $h=4$ Attention Heads:} Multi-head attention projects features into 4 independent sub-spaces, allowing different heads to concurrently track immediate trend momentum, short-term periodic fluctuations, and long-term daily patterns. This multi-head representation prevents the model from over-reacting to single-step anomaly points, ensuring steady, monotonic convergence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparative Discussion}

\subsubsection{Forecasting Accuracy Across Horizons}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_5seeds}, \textbf{TA-STGCN} consistently outperforms both GCN-LSTM and standard STGCN across every single forecast horizon ($t+1$ to $t+6$).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GCN-LSTM vs. TA-STGCN:} GCN-LSTM exhibits the highest forecasting error ($\text{MAE} = 8.5240$, $\text{RMSE} = 14.8250$) despite possessing the largest parameter count (364,198 parameters). The sequential nature of LSTM backpropagation creates a bottleneck when modeling complex 24-step historical dependencies across 608 nodes.
    \item \textbf{STGCN Baseline vs. TA-STGCN:} Standard STGCN achieves lower error than GCN-LSTM ($\text{MAE} = 3.2125$) due to parallel 1D gated temporal convolutions. However, because local 1D convolutions lack global temporal receptive fields, STGCN's error increases as the forecast horizon extends to $t+6$ ($\text{MAE}_{t+6} = 3.4345$). By incorporating Model-Level Multi-Head Temporal Self-Attention, TA-STGCN reduces overall error to $\mathbf{3.1923}$ and $t+6$ error to $\mathbf{3.4329}$.
    \item \textbf{Variance and Training Stability:} TA-STGCN exhibits significantly lower standard deviation across the 5 random seeds ($\pm 0.0112$ MAE overall compared to $\pm 0.0151$ for STGCN Baseline). This demonstrates that Model-Level Multi-Head Temporal Self-Attention enhances training stability and increases resilience against non-stationary temporal noise.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Architectural Efficiency and Parameter Economy}
A critical finding of this study is that \textbf{TA-STGCN achieves superior forecasting performance with the most lightweight parameter capacity}.
\begin{itemize}
    \item TA-STGCN requires only \textbf{165,318 parameters}, representing a \textbf{54.6\% parameter reduction} compared to GCN-LSTM (364,198 parameters) and a \textbf{45.6\% parameter reduction} compared to STGCN Baseline (303,846 parameters).
\end{itemize}
This empirical evidence proves that TA-STGCN's superior forecasting accuracy stems directly from architectural innovation—specifically coupling Chebyshev Graph Convolutions with Model-Level Multi-Head Temporal Self-Attention—rather than parameter scaling.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This paper introduced an end-to-end framework for urban traffic monitoring and multi-horizon forecasting, evaluated on a real-world camera network in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. The system decouples traffic management into two sequential sub-problems: (1) camera-level vehicle count estimation, and (2) network-wide spatio-temporal graph forecasting.

For Sub-problem 1, a ConvNeXt-Tiny direct regression network was trained on 4,000 images, validated on 1,000, and tested on 1,000 images across 608 camera locations, achieving robust vehicle count accuracy (MAE $\approx 5.0$). For Sub-problem 2, estimated vehicle counts were structured into a 608-node directed traffic graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, W)$ built with RBF-weighted road distances. Addressing the fundamental flaws of single-node independent forecasting—which ignores spatial traffic spillover—we introduced \textbf{TA-STGCN} (Temporal Attention-Guided Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network), combining Chebyshev Graph Convolutions ($K=3$), 1D Gated Temporal Convolutions (GLU), and Model-Level Multi-Head Temporal Self-Attention.

Comprehensive multi-seed evaluations across 5 independent random seeds ($42, 100, 2024, 777, 999$) demonstrated that TA-STGCN achieves state-of-the-art multi-horizon forecasting performance ($\text{MAE} = 3.1923 \pm 0.0112$, $\text{RMSE} = 4.3435 \pm 0.0142$) across all 6 prediction horizons ($t+1$ to $t+6$, 5-to-30 minutes ahead). Notably, TA-STGCN achieves these results with a significantly more lightweight parameter capacity (165,318 parameters) compared to traditional GCN-LSTM (364,198 parameters) and standard STGCN (303,846 parameters).

Future work will explore incorporating dynamic spatial graph learning to capture temporal topological shifts during major road incidents and extreme weather events.
